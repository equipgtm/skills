# PoC / pilot plan recipe — turn interest into a trial

A workshop or demo creates interest; a PoC turns it into a deal. This is the bridge: a
small, time-boxed trial in the customer's own environment with success criteria agreed *up
front*. Build it from the same outcome, so the trial proves the exact value the story
promised. Write `poc-plan.md`.

## The rule that makes or breaks it

**Agree the success criteria before the trial starts.** A PoC with a fuzzy "let's see how
it goes" never ends and never converts. Objective, agreed criteria are the whole game — the
same lesson as a loop, where a vague "done" never holds.

## Structure

- **Outcome to prove** — one measurable line, from the manifest `outcome`. "By end of week
  2, X works on your data."
- **Success criteria** — 2–4 objective, agreed checks. What the customer will see that means
  it worked.
- **Scope** — explicitly in and out. The *out* list prevents the scope creep that kills PoCs.
- **Environment & access** — their stack, the credentials or sandbox needed, who provisions
  them.
- **Plan** — milestones with dates, and *who owns each* on both sides. PoCs stall when
  ownership is vague.
- **Risks & dependencies** — what could block it, and the mitigation.
- **Exit** — what success unlocks (the commercial next step), and a clean off-ramp if it
  fails. State both.

## Rules

- **Small scope, short clock.** A PoC is a proof, not an implementation. Weeks, not quarters.
- **Objective criteria only.** If you can't show it passed or failed, it isn't a criterion.
- **One outcome.** Prove what the story promised, not five adjacent things.
- **Grounded.** Don't commit to capabilities the product doesn't have; the verifier checks
  every one you cite.
